# Aptis Speaking Topic Bank: Relationships & Family

## Task-writing principles

### Part 1

Short personal questions on familiar topics. Prompts should allow candidates to respond immediately without needing to retrieve an unusually specific experience.

### Part 2

Each task uses one photograph.

1. Describe the photograph.
2. Relate an aspect of the photograph/topic to the candidate’s own experience or context.
3. Extend the same topic into a more general opinion, explanation or judgement.

### Part 3

Each task uses two photographs showing familiar activities or scenes that can be contrasted.

1. Describe the two photographs.
2. Explore an aspect of the scenes that naturally allows contrast or comparison.
3. Express an opinion and/or preference relating to an aspect of the photographs.

Question 2 does not need to explicitly say *compare*. Prompts can instead focus on people, feelings, atmosphere, suitability, benefits, difficulty, likely experiences, etc.

### Part 4

Three linked questions form one extended response.

1. Introduce a specific personal experience.
2. Develop or reflect on that experience, normally staying closely connected to it.
3. Widen the discussion to people or society more generally.

The first question should have a relatively low “example-retrieval cost”. The difficulty should come from developing the response, not from struggling to think of a suitable event.

# Part 1 — Personal questions

1. Tell me about someone you enjoy spending time with.
2. What do you usually do when you meet your friends?
3. Who do you talk to most often during the week?
4. Tell me about someone you are close to.
5. How do you keep in touch with people who live far away?
6. Do you prefer spending time with one or two people or with a large group?
7. What do you enjoy doing with your family?
8. Tell me about a person you admire.
9. Do you find it easy to make new friends?
10. Tell me about a celebration you enjoyed.
11. Who did you spend a lot of time with when you were younger?
12. Where do you usually meet your friends?
13. What qualities do you look for in a friend?
14. How often do you see members of your extended family?
15. Do you prefer talking to people face-to-face or online?

# Part 2 — One photograph

## Task 1 — Eating together

**Photo:** A family or mixed-age group eating together at home.

1. Describe the photograph.
2. Tell me about a meal you enjoyed with family or friends.
3. Why do you think eating together is important to some families?

## Task 2 — Meeting friends

**Photo:** Friends talking together in a café or park.

1. Describe the photograph.
2. Where do you usually go when you want to spend time with friends?
3. What makes a good place for people to meet socially?

## Task 3 — Celebrating together

**Photo:** A group celebrating a birthday or another special occasion.

1. Describe the photograph.
2. Tell me about a celebration you particularly enjoyed.
3. Why do people often prefer celebrating important occasions with other people?

## Task 4 — Different generations

**Photo:** An older and younger person doing an activity together.

1. Describe the photograph.
2. Tell me about something you enjoy doing with someone from another generation.
3. What can younger and older people gain from spending time together?

## Task 5 — Keeping in touch

**Photo:** Someone having a video call with friends or relatives.

1. Describe the photograph.
2. How do you keep in contact with people you cannot see regularly?
3. Do you think technology makes it easier to maintain close relationships? Why?

## Task 6 — Helping someone

**Photo:** One person helping another with an everyday task.

1. Describe the photograph.
2. Tell me about a time when somebody helped you.
3. Why is it important for people to be willing to help each other?

## Task 7 — Shared activities

**Photo:** Friends or relatives cooking, playing a game or doing another activity together.

1. Describe the photograph.
2. What activities do you enjoy doing with other people?
3. Do shared activities help people develop stronger relationships? Why?

## Task 8 — Meeting new people

**Photo:** People meeting at a club, class or social event.

1. Describe the photograph.
2. Tell me about a situation where you met new people.
3. What are some good ways for adults to make new friends?

# Part 3 — Two photographs

## Task 1 — Family and friends

**Photo A:** A family eating together at home.
**Photo B:** Friends eating together in a café.

1. Tell me what you see in the two pictures.
2. What might people enjoy about these two ways of spending time together?
3. Which situation would you prefer for a special occasion? Why?

## Task 2 — Different generations

**Photo A:** A child spending time with an older relative.
**Photo B:** Young people spending time with friends their own age.

1. Tell me what you see in the two pictures.
2. What might young people gain from spending time with these different groups?
3. Which relationships are more important for young people: family or friends? Why?

## Task 3 — Keeping in touch

**Photo A:** Friends talking face-to-face.
**Photo B:** Friends or relatives talking on a video call.

1. Tell me what you see in the two pictures.
2. How might conversations feel different in these two situations?
3. Which way of keeping in touch is better for close relationships? Why?

## Task 4 — Social gatherings

**Photo A:** A small gathering at home.
**Photo B:** A large wedding, party or family celebration.

1. Tell me what you see in the two pictures.
2. What kind of people might enjoy these two types of gathering?
3. Which kind of gathering would you prefer for an important occasion? Why?

## Task 5 — Spending time together

**Photo A:** A family or group doing an activity together.
**Photo B:** People together in the same room but occupied with separate activities.

1. Tell me what you see in the two pictures.
2. How much interaction is there between the people in these situations?
3. Do shared activities help people build stronger relationships? Why?

## Task 6 — Old and new friends

**Photo A:** Two old friends looking at photographs or reminiscing.
**Photo B:** People meeting for the first time at an event.

1. Tell me what you see in the two pictures.
2. How might people feel in these two social situations?
3. Is it more important to keep old friends or make new ones? Why?

## Task 7 — Helping others

**Photo A:** Friends helping someone with a task.
**Photo B:** Family members helping one another.

1. Tell me what you see in the two pictures.
2. Why might people be helping each other in these situations?
3. Who should people be able to rely on more: friends or family? Why?

## Task 8 — Living arrangements

**Photo A:** Several people living or relaxing together at home.
**Photo B:** One person living or relaxing alone.

1. Tell me what you see in the two pictures.
2. What are the advantages of these two living arrangements?
3. Would you prefer to live alone or with other people? Why?

# Part 4 — Extended response

## Task 1 — Close relationships

1. Tell me about a relationship that has been particularly important to you.
2. Why has this relationship been important to you?
3. What helps close relationships last for a long time?

## Task 2 — Making a new friend

1. Tell me about a time when you became friends with someone new.
2. What helped you get to know this person?
3. Why can making new friends become more difficult as people get older?

## Task 3 — Receiving support

1. Tell me about a time when someone helped you through a difficult situation.
2. How did their help affect the situation?
3. Why do some people find it difficult to ask others for help?

## Task 4 — Keeping in touch

1. Tell me about someone you keep in contact with although you rarely see them.
2. How do you manage to keep this relationship strong?
3. How has technology changed the way people maintain long-distance relationships?

## Task 5 — Long-term friendship

1. Tell me about someone you have known for a long time.
2. How has your relationship with this person changed over time?
3. What qualities are most important in a long-term friendship?

## Task 6 — Spending time alone

1. Tell me about a time when you enjoyed spending time alone.
2. What did you particularly enjoy about that experience?
3. Why do some people need more time alone than others?

## Task 7 — Family celebrations

1. Tell me about a family celebration you particularly remember.
2. What made this occasion memorable for you?
3. Why are family celebrations important in many cultures?

## Task 8 — Helping others

1. Tell me about a time when you helped someone you know.
2. How did the person respond to your help?
3. Why are some people more willing to help others than others are?
