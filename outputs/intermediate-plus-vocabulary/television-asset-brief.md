# Intermediate Plus — Television: fresh asset brief

Decision clarified: 22 October 2026. Reuse suitable newer artwork created for Textbook Vocab, including across banks, and create coordinated new images for gaps. The older Topic Vocab collections are not the intended source. The fifteen programme targets below are a coverage list, not a requirement to regenerate suitable newer textbook assets. A WebP conversion or move into a textbook folder does not change an older image's provenance.

## Shared direction

Create separate square images, ideally at least 1254 × 1254 pixels, with a consistent polished style, lighting and level of detail. Programme pictures should look like clear, plausible stills from the programme itself, with the important people and props large enough to recognise in a small activity card. Avoid a distant television inside a furnished room: it makes the useful content too small.

Use original fictional people and programmes. Do not print programme-type names, brand names, subtitles or watermarks that give away the answer. Full scenes should retain their natural backgrounds. The optional box-set object should have a genuinely transparent background. Keep the same visual approach across the set; cartoon is the deliberate animation exception.

Images support vocabulary rather than proving every genre on their own. Brief activity clues will establish fiction versus factual reporting, live versus recorded sport, and the difference between a soap and a sitcom. Do not ask learners to infer these solely from appearance.

## Fifteen programme-image targets

| Filename | Target | Scene direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `advert.png` | advert | A polished studio product demonstration: presenter showcasing an unbranded household appliance, with the product prominent. Communicate promotion, without prices or a genre label. |
| `cartoon.png` | cartoon | An original animated story still with expressive fictional characters in a coherent setting. No recognisable franchise characters. |
| `chat-show.png` | chat show | Relaxed entertainment studio interview: host and guest in armchairs, smiling and talking, small audience suggested behind them. |
| `cookery-programme.png` | cookery programme | Presenter demonstrating a recipe at a studio kitchen counter, handling food with ingredients and utensils clearly visible. |
| `current-affairs-programme.png` | current affairs programme | A serious studio discussion with a moderator and two guests examining a public issue, supported by an unlabelled chart or contextual photograph. Visually distinct from the news bulletin. |
| `documentary.png` | documentary | A factual wildlife programme still showing penguins and their Antarctic environment in observational nature-film style. |
| `crime-drama.png` | crime drama | A cinematic fictional detective investigation: two detectives examining clues in a room, with an evidence bag and notebook. No gore; distinguish the composition from a news report. |
| `live-sport.png` | live sport | A football match in progress, with players, ball and stadium crowd clearly visible. The activity clue will establish that it is broadcast as it happens. |
| `period-drama.png` | period drama | A fictional domestic scene set around 1850, with convincing period clothes and a historical household interior. |
| `quiz-show.png` | quiz show | Bright studio competition with host, two contestants at separate podiums and prominent buzzers. Avoid written questions or answers. |
| `reality-show.png` | reality show | Participants in a shared-house kitchen, caught in an apparently unscripted discussion, with a fixed production camera visible in the corner. |
| `the-news.png` | the news | A newsreader at a broadcast desk with a world map and separate news-footage panels behind them. No genre title or readable headlines. |
| `weather-forecast.png` | weather forecast | Presenter pointing to a regional forecast map with clear sun, cloud and rain symbols. Avoid a generic outdoor weather scene. |
| `soap.png` | soap | An emotionally tense everyday conversation between recurring family or community characters in a contemporary domestic setting, with a serial-drama feel. |
| `sitcom.png` | sitcom | A staged everyday comic mishap involving recurring characters in a familiar living room or workplace, with clear amused reactions. |

## Optional object image

`box-set.png`: an unbranded coordinated slipcase holding several matching television-season disc cases, one case slightly pulled out. Simple original cover artwork, no readable title or vocabulary label. Square composition with room around the object and a transparent background.

## Build in code

- Series → seasons → episodes hierarchy, using a fictional programme and selectable examples. Context should acknowledge that British English also uses series for a run of episodes.
- A fictional streaming-service interface, populated with this new artwork.
- One coordinated television/remote interface for turn on, turn off, turn up, turn down and turn over. Show actual state changes, with no target phrase printed in scored prompts.
- A programme schedule for be on, with times and example programmes.
- A simple box-set diagram if the optional object image is not generated.

## Integration

Use WebP derivatives for runtime images, preserve source dimensions and alpha, retain supplied originals, and add no white backdrop to transparent objects. Use smaller pictures in writing rounds. Keep the bank unpublished until its assets and activities have been reviewed.
