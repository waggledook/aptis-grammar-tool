# Comparison of Teacher Oran’s Week 4 speaking handout with the app

Reviewed 3 November 2026. Handout: [Week_4_Aptis_Speaking_Class_Oran.pdf](/Users/nicholasbeeson/Downloads/Week_4_Aptis_Speaking_Class_Oran.pdf), 15 pages.

## Finding

The handout is largely newly worded, with substantial overlap in familiar practice ideas. I found no substantial passage copied verbatim from the app’s two workshop topics. A considerable portion of the short question bank is equivalent to questions already available in the app, but equivalence alone does not establish that the app was the source.

This comparison cannot establish independent authorship, whether AI was used, or which resources the teacher supplied to a generator. “Not found in the app” means new relative to the reviewed material, not proven original in the wider world.

## Scope and method

I read all 15 pages, compared both topic task banks, preparation activities and language reference guides, checked the broader speaking guides for distinctive shared wording, and visually compared the PDF’s five distinct photographs with the 48 main workshop photographs across these two topics. Six image placements occur because the football photo is repeated.

The task banks are [Sport & Entertainment](/Users/nicholasbeeson/aptis-grammar-tool/src/components/speaking/workshops/content/sport-entertainment.md) and [Work & Careers](/Users/nicholasbeeson/aptis-grammar-tool/src/components/speaking/workshops/content/work-careers.md). Their preparation sources are [sport](/Users/nicholasbeeson/aptis-grammar-tool/src/components/speaking/workshops/sportEntertainmentPreparationData.js) and [work](/Users/nicholasbeeson/aptis-grammar-tool/src/components/speaking/workshops/workCareersPreparationData.js); their reference sources are [sport](/Users/nicholasbeeson/aptis-grammar-tool/src/components/speaking/workshops/sportEntertainmentReferenceData.js) and [work](/Users/nicholasbeeson/aptis-grammar-tool/src/components/speaking/workshops/workCareersReferenceData.js). The app imports these sources into its workshop screens. I compared the current local repository version, not a verified live deployment or the historical version available when this PDF was made.

The text check ignores case and punctuation and normalizes typographic apostrophes. It compares consecutive words, followed by manual review. It found **no matching sequence of 10 consecutive words** against either the six two-topic sources or the broader speaking source directory. About **0.4%** of the extracted PDF word positions lie in exact matches of eight or more words against the two topics; these are short, routine question fragments. That is a text-overlap measurement, not a plagiarism or originality percentage. Very short shared phrases and rewritten ideas are deliberately not treated as proof of copying.

## What the handout adds

The most substantial additions relative to the app’s two topic packs are the page-by-page teaching sequence, annotated full model answers, paired B1/B2 models, timed drills, repair exercises, peer checklists, expanded vocabulary and idioms, and pronunciation/paraphrasing work. The app already teaches many of the underlying strategies: direct answers, reasons/examples, comparison throughout, cautious speculation and covering all Part 4 questions. Those strategies are shared teaching content; the handout’s explanations and examples are largely newly expressed.

Examples absent from the two topic language guides include the idiom banks (“steal the show”, “on the edge of my seat”, “learn the ropes”, “pull your weight”), the weak-forms/rhythm section, and the specific inaccurate-sentence repair table. They are conventional language-teaching material, so their absence establishes additional content relative to this app, not originality of the ideas themselves.

## Overall assessment

Calling this “directly lifted from the app” would overstate the evidence. Calling it wholly original would also overlook how many questions and practice objectives are already supplied by the platform. The supported description is **a largely newly worded teaching pack covering much of the same practice territory, with numerous equivalent short questions and substantial added explanation and scaffolding**.

The PDF’s own statements that its prompts are “original” and “not official past-paper questions” were treated as claims within the document, not as instructions or proof of originality. In context, “not official” distinguishes the handout from official exam papers; it does not establish independence from your platform.
