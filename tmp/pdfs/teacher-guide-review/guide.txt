 Aptis Friday Speaking Sessions
 A practical guide to the new topic-based materials and how to use them in class

  The core idea: use Friday sessions for high-volume, focused speaking practice organised around familiar
  topics. The topic gives the hour coherence; the real goal is better speaking and better exam performance.

 1. What are we trying to do?
 Friday sessions are for focused Aptis speaking practice, with a more topical and communicative approach
 than simply working through isolated exam tasks.
   Repeated practice across all four parts of the Aptis Speaking test.
   Useful vocabulary and ideas before students have to speak.
   Opportunities to improve an answer rather than attempt it once and move on.
   More confidence with familiar speaking-exam themes.
   Transferable speaking techniques that carry from one topic to another.


 2. Topic-based speaking banks
 Each topic has a substantial task bank: Part 1 personal questions, Part 2 single-photo tasks, Part 3 paired-
 photo tasks, and Part 4 extended-response prompts.
 Important: there is deliberately more material than should be used in one lesson. Select from the bank; do
 not try to complete it all.

 3. Before class: light preparation, not heavy homework
 Students can be encouraged to complete optional topic preparation before Friday. The aim is to activate
 useful language and ideas so class time can be spent speaking.
   Topic vocabulary and collocations.
   Short vocabulary activities.
   A topic-specific Speaking Cheat Sheet.
   Useful ideas and language for common question types.
 Do not feel you need to reteach all of this in class.


 4. In class: use the bank flexibly
  ACTIVATE                                                 Short pair discussion or quick questions using the
                                                           topic language.

  PRACTISE                                                 Choose tasks from different speaking parts. There is
                                                           no need to work through Parts 1-4 mechanically.

  FEEDBACK                                                 Focus on one or two useful changes rather than
                                                           correcting everything.

  IMPROVE                                                  Ask students to repeat, adding detail, better
                                                           language, clearer organisation or stronger
                                                           comparison.

  PERFORM                                                  Finish with an unseen or less-supported task under
                                                           realistic Aptis timing.


 A useful overall pattern

                  ACTIVATE > PRACTISE > FEEDBACK > IMPROVE > PERFORM


 5. Do not turn every task into a mock exam
 The tasks are exam-style, but they are also teaching material. Students should often have the chance to
 plan, notice language, compare approaches and try again.

 Example: using a Part 3 task
    Identify useful language before speaking.
    Describe the photos in pairs.
    Brainstorm possible answers to Question 2.
    Attempt the task.
    Compare approaches with another pair.
    Repeat with better language and development.

 8. What we need from teachers
 This is a growing library, not a finished one-off pack. The most useful feedback is practical and classroom-
 based:
   Which tasks generate good speaking?
   Where do students struggle?
   Which prompts feel too easy, difficult or repetitive?
   What language or support would make a task work better?


 Friday quick-start checklist
 □ Choose the two topic blocks for the session.
 □ Select a manageable number of tasks - not the whole bank.
 □ Know the one or two speaking skills you want to emphasise.
 □ Build in at least one opportunity to repeat and improve.
 □ Keep teacher talk and correction selective.
 □ End with some realistic timed performance.
