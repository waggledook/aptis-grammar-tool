# Aptis Listening Part 3 source review and content audit

Reviewed 23 November 2026. This note supports the original teaching extracts in [the main lesson draft](../audio-scripts/aptis-listening-part-3-strategy-lesson-draft.md) and [the more accessible variants](../audio-scripts/aptis-listening-part-3-strategy-accessible-variants-draft.md).

## Official Aptis material

The [British Council General teacher guide](https://www.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/aptis_esol_general_guide_for_teachers_2023.pdf), printed pages 18 and 21, describes four opinions attributed to the man, woman or both. Its sample concerns parents discussing children's health. The [public listening practice test](https://www.britishcouncil.org/exam/aptis/practice-materials/listening-aptis-test) was opened through the British Council's Testreach link and Question 15 inspected directly. It presents the same four opinions, covering diet, sport, quiet time and excessive sleep.

The [official listening answer key](https://www.britishcouncil.org/sites/default/files/aptis_general_-_practice_test_answer_key_listening.pdf), dated January 2024, gives Both, Man, Both and Woman for Question 15. This confirms attribution, not the wording or conversational mechanism behind each answer.

A complete official transcript for that Aptis dialogue was not located in the checked public preparation pages, teacher and candidate guides, practice interface or targeted transcript searches. The analysis below therefore does not claim to identify particular verbal traps in that recording. Third-party practice tests were not treated as official BC scripts.

The sample statements do illustrate the need to preserve a proposition's meaning and limits: a judgement about excessive sleep cannot be replaced with a judgement about sleep generally. That is an interpretation of the visible question wording, not a finding about its audio.

## Published BC teaching transcripts

These are British Council learning resources, not official Aptis examination tasks. Their scripts provide direct examples of the conversational behaviour the original extracts will teach.

### A team meeting about diversity

In [the published transcript](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/listening/b1/team-meeting-about-diversity), Brenda supports the proposed charter, then questions whether staff will pay attention to a document imposed by management. Nina responds with a participatory approach. Brenda also reports other people's negative views of diversity; that report should not be attributed to her. Later acceptance of an assigned job expresses willingness to do it, rather than a new opinion about every preceding claim.

Authoring implications: attach agreement to the particular proposition; allow reservations about implementation alongside support for an idea; distinguish reported views and practical cooperation from personal endorsement.

### Challenging ideas

In [the published transcript](https://learnenglish.britishcouncil.org/free-resources/speaking/b2/challenging-someones-ideas), Emir acknowledges Vanya's perspective while siding with Paul on the proposal's relevance. He subsequently accepts a narrower claim about attracting followers, asks about implementation and proposes a trial. Paul remains doubtful. Recognising a possible benefit or supporting an experiment does not establish confidence that the entire proposal will succeed.

Authoring implications: acknowledgement can precede disagreement; a question may challenge a proposal, so questions are not automatically neutral; track the claim attached to a short agreement response; separate willingness to test an idea from belief in its success.

## Choices for the revised bank

Ten independent conversations provide eight single-statement extracts and two extracts with two statements each. Name badges provide the opening worked model; pottery follows as the first core extract. The remaining material requires inference, interpretation of scope, separation of reported and personal views, or integration across turns. All scripts are original. The answer key contains four Man, four Woman and four Both answers, with no repeating answer cycle.

The attribution benchmark is the stronger material in the main Exercise Classes task, the teacher Children's Independence task and Mock 1 Flexible Working Hours. Examples include inferring a shared judgement from different exercise anecdotes, distinguishing routine phone tracking from emergency access, and separating a shared concern for new staff from support for working on site. The training reduces passage length and the number of statements while preserving these interpretive demands.

The lesson draft includes an editorial attribution scale from 1 to 4 and a rating for every item. One deliberately clear model is rated 1. Core items are judged 3 or 3–4; the seating item in the first extract with two statements is judged 4. These are text-based editorial estimates. They do not establish CEFR levels, empirical item difficulty or parity with the existing recordings. Confirm the comparison with natural recorded delivery and learner responses.

Students first decide whose view matches a statement. Teacher feedback describes each speaker's position as supports, opposes or no clear position. No clear position means the extract does not establish a position on that exact claim; it does not assert that the person has no opinion privately. These labels support explanation and do not add a fourth exam answer. Keep the mechanism labels and ratings hidden during independent listening.

Do not teach that every question is neutral, every positive phrase indicates agreement or every occurrence of but indicates disagreement. Include a counterexample to each shortcut in the bank or teacher feedback.

Review the draft for natural speech and unambiguous attribution before recording. The extracts are teaching materials, not calibrated exam items. Durations remain estimates until audio is recorded.

## More accessible alternatives

Six separate alternatives cover main-bank Extracts 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 9, giving seven opinion-matching items. Their editorial ratings are 2 or 2–3. They preserve the matched statements, answer keys and core distinctions, while making positions more explicit, keeping evidence closer together or removing additional competing clues. In the film discussion, the seating question is resolved before the snack question begins. The shared opening model can be used with either route.

The manuscript offers more support, more inference and mixed selections as teacher options. These are choices of material, not assigned CEFR levels or an implemented app flow. Because each pair shares its answer, a second version of the same conversation is suitable for comparison rather than a fresh assessment. Use an unheard conversation to check transfer to the harder material.
