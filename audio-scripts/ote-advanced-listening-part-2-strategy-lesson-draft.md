# OTE Advanced Listening Part 2: finding the right detail

Revised teaching edition — 11 November 2026

Eight original fictional teaching extracts: six with one gap and two with two gaps. These are short strategy exercises, not full examination tasks or calibrated C1 items.

## Aim and flexible delivery

Students predict the information required by a note, recognise paraphrase and select the precise one- or two-word noun phrase supported by the recording. Prediction concerns meaning, number, countability and specificity.

| Route | Approximate time | Extracts | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quick focus | 8–12 min | 1, 4, 8 | One model, a competing detail and an independent finish. |
| Core lesson | 15–20 min | 1, 3, 4, 5, 8 | Build from prediction and complete phrases to two ordered gaps. |
| Complete activity | 20–30 min | 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 | All eight extracts, with discussion and transcript work as needed. |

Times assume two listens and brief feedback. Select any combination, omit pair discussion when unnecessary and extend only where useful. There is no compulsory 55-minute sequence or automatic timer. The final two extracts remove guided prediction prompts.

Recorded audio is available for all 8 extracts in both classroom and live modes. Teacher-read delivery is also available. When reading or replacing a recording, use a natural pace of approximately 145–160 words per minute. Read only the script, without the title, notes, answer or teaching labels. Do not emphasise answers. The separate recording file contains all eight scripts and their filenames.

## Suggested routine

1. Preview the full note; predict a category without guessing the exact answer. Around 20 seconds for one gap or 25 seconds for two is a classroom suggestion, not an exam simulation.
2. Listen once and attempt every gap. In live mode, students submit their first-listen answers and confidence. A deliberately blank answer is allowed.
3. Optionally compare evidence briefly, without announcing the key. First responses remain unchanged.
4. Listen again and submit a separate second-listen response.
5. Optionally show the unmarked script and let students keep or revise their answers. This is recorded separately as transcript support.
6. Reveal the answer, paraphrase and competing detail; move on when the class is ready.

The teacher can skip an extract or finish early. Only submitted stages contribute to stage totals. Later attempts never replace earlier listening evidence. Correctness and speed scores are not shown during listening or comparison. Keep teacher scripts private when sharing your screen.

## Teacher scripts and keys

### Extract 1 — Designing public seating

**Main focus:** Identify the information category.

**Student prompt:** What kind of material could complete the note? Predict a category, not a word.

**Read:**

> When we design a new bench, our first concern is how it fits the space around it. A drawing can make a seat look comfortable without showing how much room someone's legs will need. So we start with small models, using cardboard because it is easy to cut and fold. These early versions let us change the angle of a backrest or the distance between seats before anyone invests time in a full-size version.

**Answer 1:** cardboard

**Prediction:** A material; an uncountable material noun can fit here.

**Evidence:** “small models, using cardboard”

**Explanation:** The note asks what the models are made from. The phrase supplies a straightforward material detail.

**Competing information:** There is no deliberate competing material in this extract.

**Discuss:** Which words helped you know what to listen for? A clear match does not need a contrast word or a distractor.

### Extract 2 — Organising volunteers

**Main focus:** Match a paraphrased purpose.

**Student prompt:** What unwanted situation does the calendar help prevent?

**Read:**

> A volunteer group can look extremely busy without making the best use of its time. In one neighbourhood project, two people independently contacted the same supplier, while another pair each prepared a list of venues. Nobody was being careless; they simply couldn't see what the others had already done. The shared calendar was introduced to prevent this duplication. Volunteers still choose their own working hours, but they can now see which jobs somebody else has taken on.

**Answer 2:** duplication

**Prediction:** An unwanted situation or problem. 'Avoid' does not by itself tell you whether the noun is uncountable or plural.

**Evidence:** “prevent this duplication”

**Explanation:** 'Prevent' matches 'avoid'. The repeated calls and lists explain duplication, but the response uses the noun heard in the recording.

**Competing information:** The calendar makes completed work visible; it does not remove volunteers' freedom to choose their hours.

**Discuss:** Did you understand the examples before recognising the noun? If so, replay the answer-bearing clause as a short dictation after feedback.

### Extract 3 — Access to museum tours

**Main focus:** Capture the complete phrase.

**Student prompt:** Read the complete note. What form of communication could a live tour be delivered in?

**Read:**

> Making a museum accessible involves more than providing written information beside the objects. Written information gives visitors considerable freedom, but it doesn't let them question a guide or follow a conversation as it develops. The museum has therefore introduced tours in sign language, led by a guide who uses it throughout. Participants can ask questions directly and respond to one another, rather than having to rely on a written summary of what the group has discussed.

**Answer 3:** sign language

**Prediction:** A language or form of communication. The complete note establishes this category, not the preposition alone.

**Evidence:** “tours in sign language”

**Explanation:** Both words identify the communication used. The opportunity to ask questions distinguishes the tour from written information.

**Competing information:** Written information and written summaries do not provide the live interaction described.

**Discuss:** What information is lost if you write only 'sign' or only 'language'? Both words are needed here; other answers may need just one.

### Extract 4 — Participation in evening classes

**Main focus:** Separate current and earlier findings.

**Student prompt:** Which words in the note tell you exactly which finding is needed?

**Read:**

> When the college first investigated low enrolment in evening classes, transport dominated the responses. Several bus routes finished before the classes did, so the timetable was changed. In this year's survey, most prospective students said the journey would now be manageable. Childcare was another matter: even with an earlier finish, they couldn't find anyone to stay with their children. A further timetable adjustment would do little to help, so the college is considering what support it could offer.

**Answer 4:** childcare

**Also accepted for this classroom activity:** child care

**Prediction:** A barrier to participation. 'This year' limits the answer; it does not predict number or word count.

**Evidence:** “In this year's survey”; “Childcare was another matter”

**Explanation:** The journey is now manageable. Finding someone to stay with the children remains a problem, so childcare is the current obstacle.

**Competing information:** Transport dominated the earlier responses, before the timetable changed.

**Response precision:** The two-word spelling 'child care' is also accepted for this classroom activity.

**Discuss:** Why was transport plausible? Explain what changed and which finding the note asks for.

### Extract 5 — Improving a study centre

**Main focus:** Connect a request to the right group.

**Student prompt:** Whose request matters here? Predict the kind of resource, without guessing its name.

**Read:**

> The study centre's consultation produced quite different requests from its users. Discussion groups wanted more chairs so they could sit in larger circles. For those working independently, extra power sockets came first: finding somewhere to connect a laptop mattered more than changing the furniture. Tutors who booked the seminar room were mainly interested in better lighting. The centre couldn't fund every request immediately, so its managers had to decide how to balance these different priorities.

**Answer 5:** power sockets

**Also accepted for this classroom activity:** sockets

**Prediction:** A resource or facility. 'Additional' alone does not force a plural noun.

**Evidence:** “For those working independently, extra power sockets came first”

**Explanation:** 'Working independently' matches 'studying on their own'; 'came first' matches 'gave the highest priority'.

**Competing information:** Chairs were requested by discussion groups; lighting was requested by tutors using the seminar room.

**Response precision:** 'Sockets' is accepted for this classroom activity because it retains the relevant meaning. 'Power sockets' is the preferred complete phrase.

**Discuss:** Connect each request to its users. A competing detail can be true and useful while answering a different question.

### Extract 6 — A neighbourhood equipment scheme

**Main focus:** Keep an early answer in mind.

**Student prompt:** What does 'established primarily' tell you to distinguish?

**Read:**

> Storage was the issue that kept coming up at the residents' meetings. People were happy to buy equipment they used only occasionally, but had nowhere to put it between uses. That's why the neighbourhood set up a central collection of tools that members could borrow and return. Membership also reduced the cost of doing small jobs. Those savings have since featured in publicity for the scheme, alongside stories of neighbours helping one another with repairs.

**Answer 6:** storage

**Prediction:** Something residents lack. The original reason matters more than later benefits.

**Evidence:** “Storage was the issue”; “had nowhere to put it between uses. That's why”

**Explanation:** The opening noun is explained before 'That's why' connects it to establishing the collection.

**Competing information:** Lower costs and neighbours helping with repairs are benefits described after the collection was set up.

**Discuss:** Which detail explains the original decision, and which details describe benefits noticed afterwards? The answer need not be the last relevant noun.

### Extract 7 — Planning sheltered seating

**Main focus:** Follow two planning decisions.

**Student prompt:** Read both notes and keep following the recording if one answer is uncertain.

**Read:**

> The first sketches placed the covered seating in a line with the square's main path. Field notes on wind direction revealed a problem with that arrangement: cold gusts would enter through the openings and pass straight across the seats. Turning the shelters offered better protection without requiring a larger roof. Pedestrian counts were useful when deciding how many seats to provide. Businesses then examined the proposed layout. Deliveries could still reach the rear service road, but two shelters stood directly in front of shop entrances. Those were moved before the plan was approved.

**Answer 7:** wind direction

**Prediction:** Information used to decide which way the shelters face.

**Evidence:** “Field notes on wind direction”; “cold gusts would enter through the openings and pass straight across the seats”; “Turning the shelters offered better protection”

**Explanation:** The field notes contain the recorded information. Cold gusts exposed a problem with the proposed arrangement, and turning the shelters addressed it. Connecting these ideas explains how wind direction informed the shelters' orientation.

**Competing information:** Pedestrian counts informed how many seats to provide. The larger roof is a design change that turning the shelters made unnecessary.

**Answer 8:** shop entrances

**Prediction:** Access points that the proposed layout would block.

**Evidence:** “two shelters stood directly in front of shop entrances”; “Those were moved before the plan was approved”

**Explanation:** The shelters blocking the entrances were moved. The note expresses the result of this change as keeping the entrances accessible.

**Competing information:** The rear service road was already accessible to deliveries; it did not require the adjustment.

**Discuss:** Explain how the field notes, cold gusts and turning the shelters connect to their orientation. Then explain what 'Those' refers to in the final sentence.

### Extract 8 — Researching a night bus service

**Main focus:** Track a group and a research method.

**Student prompt:** Read both notes. Use the second listen to check specific uncertainty.

**Read:**

> For the night bus study, we first considered hotel staff, whose employers generally arranged transport once regular services stopped. Hospital staff were more dependent on lifts from relatives or expensive taxis. It was their journeys home that we followed over the next month. Passenger counts would tell us how many people might use a new route, but not why existing options failed them. We used interviews, allowing participants to describe a journey and us to pursue unexpected points. A fixed questionnaire would have left little room for that.

**Answer 9:** hospital staff

**Prediction:** A group of travellers. Do not guess an occupation in advance.

**Evidence:** “Hospital staff were more dependent on lifts from relatives or expensive taxis”; “It was their journeys home that we followed”

**Explanation:** 'Their journeys' refers to hospital staff. Following those journeys establishes which group was studied in detail.

**Competing information:** Hotel staff were considered, but their employers generally arranged transport. 'Staff' alone cannot distinguish the groups.

**Answer 10:** interviews

**Prediction:** A method that allows researchers to follow up on what a participant says.

**Evidence:** “We used interviews”; “us to pursue unexpected points”

**Explanation:** Exploring issues raised by participants paraphrases pursuing unexpected points during the interviews.

**Competing information:** Passenger counts give numbers. A fixed questionnaire does not offer the same scope for follow-up questions.

**Discuss:** What does 'their' refer to? Which feature of the research method explains the choice? If the first answer was uncertain, did you still attend to the second?

## Reflection and follow-up

Choose one difficult gap.

I needed information about: ______________________________

I chose: ______________________________

My evidence was: ______________________________

Next time I will: ______________________________

If your answer was correct, explain what made your decision reliable.

| Difficulty | Useful next step |
|---|---|
| I misunderstood the note | Underline the words limiting time, group, purpose or relationship. Explain the question in simpler language. |
| I understood the idea but missed the spoken phrase | Replay the answer-bearing clause, do a short dictation, then compare with the script. |
| I chose the wrong group, time or purpose | Label the relationship attached to each candidate in the script. |
| I heard only part of the answer | Replay the phrase in its clause and explain what the omitted word contributes. |
| I missed the next gap | Practise shifting attention once you have entered a provisional answer. |
| I heard the answer but wrote it inaccurately | Check spelling, plural endings and the fit of the completed note. |

A prediction is a listening aid, not evidence. Not every answer follows a contrast word, and competing details can be true while describing another group, time or purpose.
