# Aptis Listening Part 3 identifying opinions in conversations

Revised draft for review, 23 November 2026.

Ten original fictional conversations train students to decide whether a particular opinion is expressed by the man, the woman or both. Eight extracts have one statement; the final two have two statements each. Every extract comes from a different conversation. One short worked model precedes extracts that require students to infer positions from experiences, explanations, recommendations and qualifications.

The main extracts aim to match the attribution demands of the stronger existing app tasks while reducing the amount of conversation and the number of statements students must track. The [source review and content audit](../docs/aptis-listening-part3-strategy-research.md) records the comparison with existing material and distinguishes official Aptis sources from BC teaching scripts. Difficulty ratings below are editorial judgements about the text, not measured learner performance or CEFR classifications.

Teachers can also choose from [six more accessible alternative scripts](aptis-listening-part-3-strategy-accessible-variants-draft.md). These match selected statements and answers in this bank and offer more local, explicit evidence for the same distinctions.

## Suggested use

Allow approximately 25–35 minutes for the complete bank with two listens and brief feedback. For a shorter lesson, use the model, a selection of core extracts and one final extract with two statements. These timings are classroom suggestions, not exam conditions.

1. Read the statement. Identify the precise judgement being made and any limits such as the group, situation or time concerned.
2. Listen once and choose Man, Woman or Both. Optionally record confidence before comparison.
3. Compare the evidence without revealing the answer. On selected extracts, ask what each speaker actually establishes about the statement. Keep the teacher's mechanism labels and difficulty ratings hidden.
4. Listen again and record a separate answer.
5. Reveal the unmarked transcript if further support is useful, then the key and evidence for each speaker. Keep an answer reached with transcript support separate from the listening attempts.

No clear position means the extract does not establish that person's view on the exact statement. It is different from disagreement, and it does not mean the person has no private opinion. It is a teaching label rather than an additional exam response.

Choose Both only when there is evidence that both speakers express the target opinion. A question, acknowledgement, reported view or agreement about a different point is insufficient on its own. Questions can also express criticism; decide from their meaning in context.

For each statement, choose **Man**, **Woman** or **Both**. Decide whose opinion matches the statement, rather than who mentions the subject first.

## Teacher scripts and feedback

Read only the dialogue aloud. Keep titles, statements, answers and teaching notes out of the recording. Use two distinguishable voices at a natural conversational pace. Do not signal the answer through exaggerated emphasis, long pauses before decisive words or a sarcastic delivery.

The worked model is roughly 20 seconds. Most other single-statement extracts are intended to last roughly 30–45 seconds, and the final two roughly 55–75 seconds. Confirm timing and intelligibility after recording. Explain unfamiliar setting vocabulary beforehand if needed, without revealing anyone's opinion.

### Extract 1 Name badges at a social event

**Statement:** Name badges make it easier to start conversations with unfamiliar people.

**Dialogue**

**Woman:** I was relieved they gave us name badges at that neighbourhood evening. I never know how to approach someone I've just met.

**Man:** Me too. Having a name to use makes that first sentence much less awkward.

**Woman:** Exactly. After that, you can usually find something else to talk about.

**Answer:** Both.

**Positions:** Both support the statement.

**Answer notes**

- **Man — expresses this opinion:**
  - A name makes the opening sentence less awkward.
- **Woman — expresses this opinion:**
  - “Exactly” endorses that same benefit.

**Key distinction:** Different wording; the same opinion.

**Likely error:** Choosing only the woman because she introduces the topic.

**Teaching use:** This is the one deliberately clear worked model. Establish that a shared opinion may be worded differently by each speaker, then move to the less explicit exchanges.

### Extract 2 A pottery workshop

**Statement:** The pottery workshop costs more than it is worth.

**Dialogue**

**Man:** Thirty-five pounds, and I spent half the workshop waiting for the wheel. I could have bought a bowl like mine for eight.

**Woman:** Mine wouldn't sell for eight pence. But I booked another afternoon with the same potter, at the same price. By the end, I'd made something without her touching it.

**Man:** That was satisfying. If we'd had another half-hour actually making things, I might have joined you.

**Answer:** Man.

**Positions:** Man supports the statement. Woman opposes it through her evaluation of the experience.

**Answer notes**

- **Man — expresses this opinion:**
  - £35, but half the workshop spent waiting.
  - More making time might persuade him to book again.
- **Woman — does not share this opinion:**
  - Books again at the same price.
  - Values making something independently, despite the poor bowl.

**Key distinction:** Criticising the bowl is different from judging the workshop poor value.

**Likely errors:** Choosing Both because both criticise their bowls, or excluding the man because he also found making something satisfying.

**Discuss:** What would need to change before the man booked again? What has the woman paid for beyond the finished bowl?

### Extract 3 A planetarium visit

**Statement:** The planetarium seats are uncomfortable.

**Dialogue**

**Woman:** The stars looked amazing. I missed some of the final section, though; I was too busy trying to find a way to sit.

**Man:** A couple of reviewers said they couldn't get comfortable either. Was that about the distance between the rows or the way the seats lean back?

**Woman:** For me, the backrest. I ended up perched on the front edge and was glad when we could stand up.

**Man:** Does the booking plan show whether all the rows have that design? I'm choosing tickets for next week.

**Answer:** Woman.

**Positions:** Woman supports the statement. Man leaves his own assessment unclear.

**Answer notes**

- **Man — no clear position on this point:**
  - Reports the reviewers’ discomfort; gives no assessment of his own.
  - Questions about seat design do not establish agreement.
- **Woman — expresses this opinion:**
  - Struggles to find a way to sit.
  - Perches on the edge; feels relieved to stand up.

**Key distinction:** Reporting a complaint is different from expressing it as your own view.

**Likely error:** Treating a report of other people's complaints, or a practical question prompted by them, as personal endorsement.

**Discuss:** What is the source of each speaker's information? Which speaker actually evaluates sitting in the seats?

### Extract 4 Booking a haircut

**Statement:** Customers who have always kept their appointments should not have to pay the booking deposit.

**Dialogue**

**Woman:** Six years I've been going to Rosa, and I've never missed an appointment. Now she won't put me in the diary until I've transferred ten pounds.

**Man:** My sister calls that paying for nothing, although it comes off the bill. And Rosa can't sell the same hour again if someone doesn't turn up.

**Woman:** For someone who's let her down, perhaps. After six years, I shouldn't have to prove I'll come.

**Man:** Then she'd have to tell one regular customer their promise was enough and another that it wasn't. I'd rather she kept the same arrangement for everyone.

**Answer:** Woman.

**Positions:** Woman supports an exception for established, reliable customers. Man opposes that exception.

**Answer notes**

- **Man — does not share this opinion:**
  - The initial criticism belongs to his sister.
  - His own preference: the same deposit rule for everyone.
- **Woman — expresses this opinion:**
  - Has never missed an appointment.
  - Objects to proving she will attend after six reliable years.

**Key distinction:** She wants an exception for reliable customers, not an end to all deposits.

**Likely errors:** Attributing the sister's view to the man; treating the woman's limited acceptance of deposits as acceptance of this policy for regular customers; overlooking the referent of everyone.

**Discuss:** Which customers would the woman treat differently, and why does the man resist making that distinction?

### Extract 5 Meal kits

**Statement:** Meal kits reduce the time needed to prepare an evening meal.

**Dialogue**

**Man:** The recipe card says forty minutes. Same as my usual dinner, apparently. It doesn't mention that I used to spend another twenty finding ingredients and measuring everything out.

**Woman:** I didn't order a second box. Too much money for two meals. Though on Tuesday I had the vegetables in the pan before my husband had finished getting changed. That never happens when I start from the cupboard.

**Man:** I still had the washing-up afterwards.

**Woman:** So did I. The little bags don't solve that.

**Answer:** Both.

**Positions:** Both support the statement about preparation time.

**Answer notes**

- **Man — expresses this opinion:**
  - The card’s 40 minutes initially sounds unchanged.
  - His usual routine also needed 20 minutes finding and measuring ingredients.
- **Woman — expresses this opinion:**
  - Gets the vegetables into the pan sooner than in her normal routine.
  - Stops ordering over price, not preparation time.

**Key distinction:** Same cooking time and later washing-up do not cancel a preparation-time saving.

**Likely errors:** Using the cooking time on the card as the whole comparison; treating cancellation over price as rejection of the time benefit; letting the closing complaint replace the target judgement.

**Discuss:** What belongs in the man's before-and-after comparison? Which part of the woman's experience matters to this statement?

### Extract 6 A new escape room

**Statement:** The new escape room is a good choice for a group of complete beginners.

**Dialogue**

**Man:** I'm taking the accounts team to the new escape room. Not one of them has done one before. We got through it without needing any history or maths.

**Woman:** I liked that about it. But nobody told us we could ask for a hint until you pressed the button. You knew what it was for.

**Man:** The host explains it if you ask.

**Woman:** That's the problem: we knew what to ask. I'd save this one for their second visit.

**Answer:** Man.

**Positions:** Man supports the statement through his proposed choice and reason. Woman opposes starting complete beginners there.

**Answer notes**

- **Man — expresses this opinion:**
  - Chooses the room for a team who have never tried one.
  - No history or maths needed: his reason for that choice.
- **Woman — does not share this opinion:**
  - Approves of the puzzle content, not its suitability for newcomers.
  - Beginners may not know to ask for hints; wants them to try another room first.

**Key distinction:** No specialist knowledge needed is different from no previous escape-room experience needed.

**Likely error:** Extending the woman's initial agreement to the man's recommendation without identifying what that refers to.

**Discuss:** What kind of knowledge is each speaker considering? Why doesn't the offer of an explanation settle her concern?

### Extract 7 A personalised present

**Statement:** A personalised present is worth waiting longer for.

**Dialogue**

**Woman:** Dad's birthday is Monday. The mug with a drawing of his old boat won't arrive for three weeks. I've ordered it anyway; he'll know I haven't just grabbed the first thing on a shelf.

**Man:** The website says that's why people keep them. Do you send the picture yourself or choose one of their designs?

**Woman:** I've sent a photo from years ago. Otherwise I could just get an ordinary mug tomorrow.

**Man:** Do the three weeks include approving the drawing? My colleague was looking at that company for a retirement present.

**Answer:** Woman.

**Positions:** Woman supports the statement. Man leaves his position on accepting a longer wait unclear.

**Answer notes**

- **Man — no clear position on this point:**
  - The claim about keeping the gift comes from the website.
  - Asks about ordering and timing; does not judge the extra wait.
- **Woman — expresses this opinion:**
  - Orders despite the three-week delay.
  - Chooses personal meaning over an ordinary mug available tomorrow.

**Key distinction:** Interest in the gift or ordering process does not establish acceptance of the wait.

**Likely errors:** Treating the seller's claim as the man's opinion; interpreting detailed interest in the ordering process as approval of the woman's trade-off.

**Discuss:** What disadvantage has the woman accepted, and what compensates for it? Does the man make that comparison himself?

### Extract 8 Telephone menus

**Statement:** Callers should be able to select a known option without hearing the whole telephone menu.

**Dialogue**

**Man:** With my bank, I know it's option four, but pressing it early just starts the recording again. By the time I reach it, I've heard about mortgages, savings and travel insurance. I'm only trying to report a damaged card.

**Woman:** The explanations at my electricity company are longer. I wouldn't cut them; my dad needs every step. I just press three as soon as they start.

**Man:** That's what my bank used to allow. Now I get punished for remembering the number.

**Woman:** My dad and I don't need to take the same route to reach the same department.

**Answer:** Both.

**Positions:** Both support the statement.

**Answer notes**

- **Man — expresses this opinion:**
  - Knows option four but cannot select it early.
  - Calls that restriction being “punished” for remembering the number.
- **Woman — expresses this opinion:**
  - Selects her own option as soon as the recording starts.
  - Wants different routes for herself and her dad, who needs the explanations.

**Key distinction:** Keeping explanations available is different from making every caller hear them.

**Likely errors:** Treating the woman's defence of long explanations as opposition to a shortcut; expanding the claim into removing recorded explanations altogether.

**Discuss:** Why can the woman defend the explanations and still share the preference in the statement?

### Extract 9 An outdoor film screening

**Statements**

- **9a:** Seats should be reserved when tickets are booked.
- **9b:** Sellers should bring snacks to people in their seats while the film is playing.

**Dialogue**

**Woman:** I'd put a seat number on each booking for the outdoor screening. Friends could choose a row together before they came.

**Man:** I'd have left people to choose on arrival. And someone could take snacks round during the film, so nobody missed a scene buying something.

**Woman:** Last year, saving chairs caused arguments. We needed two volunteers just to sort them out. As for the snacks, how would anyone see past the seller while the change was being counted?

**Man:** People could pay when they book. The seller would only have to hand things over.

**Woman:** That takes the money out of it. I'd still spend part of the scene looking at someone's back.

**Man:** On the seating, let people pick their seat on the booking page. They keep a choice, and the volunteers can actually watch the film this time.

**Answers:** 9a Both; 9b Man.

**9a answer notes**

- **Man — expresses this opinion:**
  - Initially favours choosing on arrival.
  - Later proposes choosing on the booking page to avoid the arguments.
- **Woman — expresses this opinion:**
  - Proposes a seat number for each booking.

**Key distinction:** Use his revised view, not his first preference.

**9b answer notes**

- **Man — expresses this opinion:**
  - Proposes bringing snacks round during the film.
  - Advance payment is his way to make that service work.
- **Woman — does not share this opinion:**
  - Her question challenges the blocked view.
  - Accepts removing cash payments; still objects to someone in front of the screen.

**Key distinction:** Accepting a payment improvement does not mean accepting the snack service.

**Likely errors:** Keeping the man's first seating preference as his final position; interpreting the woman's question as neutral information-seeking; treating her acceptance of advance payment as agreement with the snack service; applying one opinion to both statements.

**Discuss after both answers:** How does the man's last turn alter the seating answer? Which part of the snack proposal remains objectionable after the payment issue is addressed?

### Extract 10 Looking after cats

**Statements**

- **10a:** Home visits would be the first choice for their cat.
- **10b:** Friends should be given money for looking after someone's pets.

**Dialogue**

**Man:** At the boarding place, Leo hid under a blanket. Put a cat somewhere familiar and you've removed a lot of that. A neighbour with a key would be my starting point.

**Woman:** Mine stayed home last summer. Two visits a day left him alone most of the time. At the boarding place, there's company and someone to notice problems.

**Man:** Does Amira still help if you can't get a place there?

**Woman:** Yes. She wouldn't name a price, but I left forty pounds. I'd budget for that whoever took the key. They're giving up their time, even if they live next door.

**Man:** Tom takes our key, and I keep weekends free to look after his cat. We don't count the visits.

**Woman:** Amira hardly travels. I wouldn't have her doing it year after year without getting something back.

**Man:** If that changed, I'd take Tom for a meal. Handing over cash would make it feel like booking a service whenever I asked.

**Answers:** 10a Man; 10b Woman.

**10a answer notes**

- **Man — expresses this opinion:**
  - Familiar surroundings reduce the cat’s distress.
  - Makes a neighbour with a key his starting point.
- **Woman — does not share this opinion:**
  - Values company and observation at the boarding place.
  - Confirms home visits as the fallback when a boarding place is unavailable.

**Key distinction:** Using home visits as a fallback is different from preferring them.

**10b answer notes**

- **Man — does not share this opinion:**
  - Returns the favour through cat care; would offer a meal instead.
  - Cash would make asking a friend feel like booking a service.
- **Woman — expresses this opinion:**
  - Leaves £40 even though her friend names no price.
  - Would budget money for whoever takes responsibility.

**Key distinction:** Both favour giving something back; only the woman favours giving money.

**Likely errors:** Treating a fallback arrangement as the first choice; confusing recognition of a friend's effort with support for payment.

**Discuss after both answers:** What does each speaker prioritise when choosing care? What obligation do they share, and where do their preferred ways of meeting it differ?

## Attribution difficulty and answer overview

Use this scale for teacher review, not as a student level label:

- **1 Direct:** positions are expressed openly, often with an agreement formula.
- **2 Straightforward:** simple paraphrase or one clear contrast establishes the answer.
- **3 Demanding:** attribution requires inference, the scope of an acknowledgement or comparison, or separation of a reported view from a personal one.
- **4 Challenging:** several clues or positions must be integrated while resolving a credible competing interpretation. Length alone does not establish this rating.

| Item | Answer | Editorial rating | Attribution demand |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Both | 1 | Worked model with explicit agreement and paraphrase |
| 2 | Man | 3 | Both criticise an object; they value the experience differently |
| 3 | Woman | 3 | Experienced discomfort versus engagement with reported complaints |
| 4 | Woman | 3–4 | Reported criticism, limited acceptance and an argument for a uniform policy |
| 5 | Both | 3 | Reconstruct the time comparison and retain it despite price and washing-up complaints |
| 6 | Man | 3–4 | Shared approval of puzzle content masks different views about knowing the activity's conventions |
| 7 | Woman | 3 | Infer an accepted trade-off; separate the seller's claim and practical questions from endorsement |
| 8 | Both | 3 | Defence of full explanations does not reject a shortcut for callers who know the option |
| 9a | Both | 4 | Revise an initial attribution using a later proposal and its rationale after an intervening topic |
| 9b | Man | 3–4 | Interpret a challenging question and distinguish a partial concession from acceptance of the proposal |
| 10a | Man | 3–4 | Compare different priorities and distinguish a fallback arrangement from a preference |
| 10b | Woman | 3–4 | Shared recognition of a commitment leads to different views about money and reciprocity |

The main comparison is with the harder attribution questions in Exercise Classes, Children's Independence and the flexible-working mock. Those require listeners to connect anecdotes with a judgement, retain the scope of partial agreement or distinguish a shared concern from a shared solution. The same operations are required here with fewer statements and shorter passages. Actual parity remains to be checked with recorded audio and learner responses.

## Recording review

Check that the voices are easy to distinguish, each opening supplies enough context and no answer depends on delivery alone. On the final extracts, keep normal conversational transitions; a conspicuous pause at every statement boundary would remove some of the tracking demand.

For every key, identify evidence of the target view for each included speaker. For an excluded speaker, identify opposition, a narrower or different claim, or the absence of a clear position. Treat Both as a serious alternative during review. If it remains defensible from the exact wording, sharpen the script or statement rather than relying on an explanation to reject it.

After recording, compare a core extract with a short section from a stronger existing task at a similar pace. Pilot first-listen and second-listen answers separately, and ask for the evidence behind them. Correct answers reached through a shortcut do not demonstrate the intended skill; disagreements about a defensible alternative may indicate an item problem rather than useful difficulty.

Avoid displaying the extract's teaching mechanism before the first listen. After feedback, use a short replay or optional dictation to examine difficult wording; dictation is supplementary and should not replace opinion matching as the main task.
